Production incidents are stressful. There's pressure to fix things fast, multiple people asking for updates, and the temptation to try random things until something works. Over the years, I've developed a framework that keeps me calm and effective.

## Step 1: Reproduce, don't guess

The single biggest mistake I see engineers make is jumping to solutions before they understand the problem. Resist the urge. Your first job is to reproduce the issue reliably.

If you can't reproduce it, you can't verify your fix. You're just guessing.

## Step 2: Narrow the blast radius

Before you debug, contain the damage. Can you feature-flag it off? Roll back the deploy? Redirect traffic? The goal is to stop the bleeding while you investigate.

A quick mitigation buys you time to find the real fix instead of shipping a panic patch.

## Step 3: Gather evidence, then form hypotheses

Look at what changed recently:

- **Deployments** — what shipped in the last 24 hours?
- **Dependencies** — did anything upstream change?
- **Traffic patterns** — is this load-related?
- **Error logs** — what's the actual stack trace?

Form a hypothesis based on evidence, not intuition. "It's probably the database" is not a hypothesis. "The connection pool is exhausted because the new query doesn't have a timeout" is.

## Step 4: Verify before you ship

Once you have a fix, test it against the reproduction case from step 1. Don't skip this because you're in a hurry. A bad fix on top of a production issue makes everything worse.

## Step 5: Write the postmortem

Not because someone told you to. Because future-you will encounter a similar issue and wish you'd written down what happened.

A good postmortem answers three questions:

1. What happened?
2. Why did it happen?
3. What will we do to prevent it?

---

The engineers I trust most in an incident aren't the fastest — they're the most methodical. Speed comes from having a good process, not from panicking faster.
